Methodology & data sources

Where the numbers come from

Standings and official qualification data are read from the English Wikipedia article {{ season }} ATP Finals through Wikipedia's public API, once every six hours. The article's wikitext is parsed rather than its rendered page, because the markup is more stable. Source: {{ source }}, parser {{ parser_version }}.

ATP's own site refuses automated clients, and this project does not disguise itself or bypass any access control to get around that. Wikipedia is used instead because its content is openly licensed for reuse, its API is intended for programmatic access, and it carries a per-tournament points ledger plus a citation for every announced qualification.

The per-player points breakdown

Each row in the table expands to show the tournaments that make up that player's race total. The article records those as a grid: four Grand Slam columns, eight mandatory Masters 1000 columns, and up to six “best other” results. Each cell is read from its own wikilink rather than from the column it sits in, which matters because the rulebook lets a player replace up to three mandatory Masters 1000 results with other next-best results. Where that has happened the breakdown names the tournament actually played and the mandatory slot it was counted in, in italics, as the article does — reading the column instead would report a tournament the player never entered.

Two things the breakdown deliberately does not claim:

  • It is not a full schedule. Only a player's best results count toward the race, so the breakdown lists fewer tournaments than the season they have played. The summary line states both numbers.
  • A skipped event and an unplayed one are different. A mandatory event the player did not enter is shown as A scoring zero; one that has not been held yet is shown as a dash and scores nothing at all.

A breakdown is shown only if its points add up to exactly the total the article states for that row. One that does not reconcile is dropped on its own: the row keeps the published total and simply offers no breakdown, so a displayed total is always one you can verify by adding up the lines. Each tournament links to the article for that draw.

Freshness — this page is not live

Wikipedia publishes these standings weekly and states the date they are current to. That stated date is what the page shows as the source date, and the page never claims to be more current than its source. Once the data is older than the configured threshold the page labels itself stale — last known good rather than implying freshness it does not have.

In-tournament projections (points after one more win, maximum reachable this week) are published only by the ATP. Rather than show columns of blanks, this site omits them.

Player links

Each player's name links to their profile on atptour.com. Nothing is fetched from there — the link is the only interaction this site has with the ATP's site. The profile ids come from Wikidata property P536, which publishes them as structured data in the public domain (CC0), keyed by the same Wikipedia article titles used here as player identity. The map is resolved offline and compiled into the binary, and the ids are format-checked before they are turned into URLs. A player with no id on Wikidata is shown unlinked rather than linked to a guess.

What is computed here

  • Provisional selection — ruleset {{ ruleset }} applied to the parsed table: the top seven by race rank, then up to two current-year Grand Slam champions ranked 8–20 in ranking order, then everyone else in rank order. The first eight unique players are the provisional selections and the ninth is the first alternate. Labelled “if the season ended now.”
  • Margins — signed points distance to the qualification line, suppressed when the Grand Slam champion provision is active because no single contiguous line exists then.
  • Movement — derived by comparing this snapshot with our own previous stored snapshot, not taken from any source.

Official versus provisional

Officially announced qualification is never inferred from points. Each announcement is ingested with the URL that announced it and stored alongside the snapshot; provisional selection is always computed and always labelled as such.

Integrity

Every fetch produces a candidate snapshot that must pass structural validation — the table must be present, ranks contiguous and unique, points non-increasing, and any announced qualifier must appear in the standings. A candidate that fails is rejected wholesale and can never replace the stored snapshot. Snapshots are append-only and immutable; corrections are new versions.

The points breakdown is checked per row rather than per article, because it is secondary to the standings: a breakdown that does not reconcile is discarded while the row it belongs to is still published. That keeps a cosmetic change upstream from withdrawing the whole table, and the number of rows affected is logged so the failure cannot pass unnoticed.
