Methodology

Source

Standings and announced qualifications are read from the Wikipedia article {{ season }} ATP Finals through its public API every six hours, parsing the wikitext rather than the rendered page. ATP's own site refuses automated clients, and this project does not disguise itself or bypass that. Wikipedia's content is openly licensed, its API is meant for programmatic access, and it carries a per-tournament points ledger plus a citation for every announced qualification.

Freshness

Wikipedia publishes the standings weekly with a stated as-of date. That date is what the page shows, and it never claims to be more current. Past the configured threshold the page labels itself stale rather than implying freshness it does not have. In-tournament projections are published only by the ATP, so they are omitted.

What is computed here

  • Provisional selection — the top seven by race rank, then up to two {{ season }} Grand Slam champions ranked 8–20, then everyone else in rank order. The first eight are the provisional qualifiers and the ninth is first alternate. Official qualification is never inferred from points: each announcement is ingested with the URL that made it.
  • Margins — signed points distance to the qualification line, hidden when the Grand Slam champion provision is active because no single line exists then.
  • Movement — a comparison with our own previous snapshot, not taken from any source.

Points breakdown

Each row expands to the tournaments behind its total, as the article records them: four Grand Slams, eight mandatory Masters 1000s, and up to six best other results. Each cell is read from its own link rather than its column, because a player may replace up to three mandatory Masters results with next-best results. Those appear in italics, naming the event played and the slot it filled, as the article does.

  • Only a player's best results count, so the list is shorter than their season. The summary line gives both numbers.
  • A skipped mandatory event shows A and scores zero; one not yet held shows a dash.
  • A breakdown is shown only if it adds up to the row's stated total. One that does not is dropped while the row keeps its published total.

Player links

Names link to atptour.com profiles; nothing is fetched from there. Profile ids come from Wikidata property P536 (CC0), resolved offline and compiled in. A player with no id is left unlinked rather than linked to a guess.

Integrity

Every fetch must pass structural validation — table present, ranks contiguous and unique, points non-increasing, every announced qualifier present in the standings — or it is rejected wholesale. Snapshots are append-only; corrections are new versions. Breakdowns are checked per row, so a cosmetic change upstream cannot withdraw the whole table, and the number of rows affected is logged.
